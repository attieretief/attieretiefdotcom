Threads

A family record is more than a chain of names. The longer you sit with the documents, the more you notice resonances — names that carry, dates that echo, places where two different families' paths happened to cross long before they had any reason to know each other. What follows is what has caught the eye so far, with the documents to back it up.

  1. One piece of paper that names three generations

    1843

    The 1843 Death Notice of Andries Johannes Retief — a single sheet at the Master's office that anchors Gen 4, 5 and 6 at once, and confirms the farm name Windhoek from the clerk's own hand.

  2. The generation WikiTree dropped — and the family remembered

    1817–1843

    Public trees quietly skipped Gen 5. The family kept them. The 1837 DRC marriage, the 1839 Swartland baptism, and the 1843 estate all say the family was right.

  3. Two 14 Junes, fifty years apart

    1924–1974

    Attie's grandmother was born 14 June 1924. Attie was born 14 June, fifty years later. His mother closed the gap almost exactly in the middle.

  4. The recurring names — three and three

    Two stretches where the same name carries straight down three generations. Andries Johannes at Gen 5–6–7; Adriaan Albertus at Gen 8–9–10. Six men, two names, six consecutive generations.

  5. The Sussex family that landed at Piketberg

    1790–1837

    James Laker was christened in Rudgwick, Sussex in 1790. By 1819 the family had crossed to the Cape — and specifically to Piketberg, the exact small farming district the Retiefs had been working since the early 1700s.

  6. Two English settler threads, on either side

    The family runs back to two different small English villages — Rudgwick in Sussex on the paternal side, Bodham in Norfolk on the maternal. Either would be unusual for an Afrikaans family. Both is striking.

  7. The 1840s Frost–Terblanche blend

    1840s

    Norfolk and the Karoo, in the same household by the 1840s. William Doughty Frost married Susanna Magdalena Terblanche — English settler and Afrikaans trekboer blending early, long before it was the norm.

  8. Two 1951 Free State weddings — both of Izelle's grandparents

    1951

    Same year, same province, both couples: 5 May 1951 Koffiefontein (du Plessis × Meades) and 29 December 1951 Verkeerdevlei (Tait × Duvenage). Without those two OFS weddings, the daughters' side of the family does not exist.

  9. The Petronella thread

    On Izelle's maternal side the middle name Petronella carries straight down: Ammerentia Petronella Duvenage → Johanna Petronella Tait. Three generations quietly threaded together by a single middle name.

  10. The Fourie middle name — mother or martyr?

    1914

    Jopie du Plessis carries a Fourie middle name. Once thought to point at a Fourie mother up the line, it now looks more like commemoration of the Boer martyr Jopie Fourie, added at baptism in the weeks after the martyr's December 1914 execution.

  11. A 1942 driver's licence as anchor

    1942–1955

    Two family artefacts — a 1942 Johannesburg driver's licence and a 1955 SA identity card — both match the FamilySearch profile of Attie's grandfather exactly. As solid as 20th-century genealogy gets without a parish register.

  12. The Scottish Borders, eight generations back

    c. 1740–1928

    The Tait line climbs seven generations from Pieter Johannes Tait to George Tait (b. 1765) and his father Joshua. Trumbull, Nesbit, Johnston, Barr — the Scottish Borders cluster, full stop.

  13. The Piet Retief near-miss

    1663–1838

    The family is not descended from the Voortrekker leader Piet Retief, but shares his stamvader. Both lines run back to François Retif (b. Mer, 1663) and diverge at Gen 2 — this family through Paul, the Piet Retief line through a separate son.

  14. Survival at Windhoek

    1843

    Andries Johannes Retief (Gen 5) died at twenty-five. His son and namesake was six when the estate was inventoried. The whole line continues only because the boy — and his sister — survived 1843.

  15. Three Marthinus Petrus du Plessis's

    1878–1952

    The du Plessis side has its own name-carrying stretch. Marthinus Petrus (1878, Swellendam) → Marthinus Petrus Jr. (1908, Luckhoff) → Martinis Petrus (1952, Koffiefontein). Three generations of the same first two names, across seventy-four years.

  16. A Kimberley diamond-fields wedding: Meades × Scholtz, 1883

    1883–1915

    Izelle's Meades-line great-great-grandparents were married on 17 September 1883 at Dutoit's Pan — one of the alluvial-diamond diggings that had by then grown into the town of Kimberley. Their granddaughter Francina Meades inherited her grandmother's full given names.

  17. Johan Wilhelm Albertus, two generations apart

    1864–1920

    Attie's maternal grandfather, Johan Wilhelm Albertus van Zyl (b. 1920), carries the full three-name Christian block of his own maternal grandfather Johan Wilhelm Albertus van Niekerk (1864–1938). Grandfather's father to grandson, preserved intact.

Honest open questions

A record is more trustworthy when it admits what it doesn't know. Four questions are standing open at the time of writing.

  • The Saayman / Krotoa test. The Saayman line climbs two clean DRC generations from Maria Margaretha Saayman (1906) up through Jacobus Ernst Saayman (1878) to Johannes Mattheus Saayman × Maria Margaretha Kotze. The next rung up — Johannes Mattheus's parents — would settle whether this branch joins the Daniel Zaaijman / Krotoa root, or a different progenitor entirely. Either answer matters.
  • The Van Zyl bridge. Jacobus Lodewyk van Zyl (1879) carries twelve sources on his FamilySearch profile but no parents. The stamvader Willem van Zyl of Vrede en Lust (1668 Delft → 1699 Cape) is documented at the top; the four or five rungs between 1879 and 1699 are not yet bridged in the public tree.
  • The Fuller immigrant. The Fuller surname climbs cleanly to Charles Henry Fuller (1828–1872) and stops. His profile has only two sources; his parents are not recorded. The original England-born Fuller who arrived at the Cape has not yet been found.
  • The du Plessis middle. The line is anchored at the top by the noble Huguenot stamvader Jean Prieur du Plessis (1638, Poitiers; Oosterland 1688), and now at the bottom by Jopie du Plessis (~1914, Koffiefontein) and his father Marthinus Petrus du Plessis. The middle generations between them are not yet fully traced.

Each open question is a row you might one day add to this site by writing one new content file. That is the point of building the record this way.
